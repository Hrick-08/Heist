ONE CAMPUS,
REWRITTEN

Four panels and you know the day. Everything after this is detail.

Turn the campus into a game world for a day: missions, money, police and a city that keeps score.

“Register in a crew. Run the missions. Watch the board — because the crews at the top of it become the crews the police are sent to find.”

TOP 3
BECOME TARGETS

What this is, honestly

Students register in crews of 4–5 and spend a day running short missions at stations across campus: puzzles, tech challenges, creative tasks, physical team games and clue hunts. Every mission pays points. Some crews end up wanted — and wanted crews have to decide whether to pay Dexter or eat the penalty.

This is not a gaming tournament and nobody plays Grand Theft Auto on a screen. The campus is the map, the day is the clock, the missions are run by hand. A web platform built by GDG keeps the points, the target list, the clearances and the leaderboard honest while you work.

crews of 4–5free entry6.5 hours3 rounds + finalevery branch welcomeno rig, no console

Being at the top of the board is the goal and the problem at the same time. Winning the round puts your face on the target list.

  • 4–5students per crew, one crew ID each
  • 3mission rounds, same set for every crew
  • 5mission categories, one scoring sheet
  • 5crews reach the final heist. Only five.
File 02

ASSEMBLE
A CREW

Nobody registers as an individual and nobody registers as police. You come as a crew, you leave as a crew ID on a board.

  1. 01

    Pick four or five, and a name

    A crew is 4–5 students, each with a valid Chitkara ID. The name is your identity all day — board, posters, target list, awards. Pick one a speaker can read loudly.

    // one registration = one crew = one crew ID

  2. 02

    Get checked in, get your ID

    At registration the crew receives a team ID and hears the story, the schedule and the rules. That ID is how a station verifies you, how points land in your total and how the control team knows who to send the police to.

    // photograph it. a dead phone is a lost day otherwise.

  3. 03

    Name a team leader for the poster

    If your crew lands on the target list, the police take a photo of your team leader and upload it to the event site. The site links that photo to your crew ID and publishes the wanted poster. That photo is used only for the internal wanted board and it is deleted after the event.

    // would rather not be photographed? Say so at registration — see rule 9

  4. 04

    Take the same hand as everyone else

    All crews get the same missions, the same time limits and the same opening wallet of in-game cash. Nothing is hidden from you: scoring criteria are published before the event, and every station uses the same sheet.

    // opening wallet: $1,000 in-game (proposed · final number set by control)

  5. 05

    Spend money like it means something

    In-game cash is not your score — points are. Cash is what you spend: to clear a wanted status at Dexter's desk, to buy a hint, to win the strategy missions that ask you to choose between a safe return and a greedy one.

    // the strategy is real even though the money is not

  6. 06

    Watch your own file

    Your crew page is live from the first entry: points, cash, missions completed, hints used, wanted status, the poster if you have one, and your position on the board. So is every other crew's.

    // the board is public. Your decisions are public. Behave accordingly.

THE CITY
HAS EMPLOYEES

Every role you meet is played by a member of the organising team. Nobody improvises the rules, and nobody touches you.

Police

Vice City PD volunteer officers

Game operators, not law enforcement. They patrol only the approved event areas, check a crew ID off the current target list, ask that crew to pause, photograph the team leader, and mark the status in the site.

  • Patrol designated areas only
  • Verify the ID against the target list
  • Take and upload the leader photo
  • Set wanted status in the platform
  • Never chase, grab, block or run
Dexter

The Broker one desk, one queue

The only place a wanted crew can fix itself. Dexter issues special missions and bonus tasks, announces round updates, takes your 100 in-game cash and removes your wanted status on the spot.

  • Clear wanted status for the fee
  • Issue special and bonus missions
  • Read out round updates
  • Log every payment and status change
Stations

Mission coordinators one per category

They explain the task in ninety seconds, run the clock, score you on the published sheet, and check whether you walked in wanted — before the mission starts, not after.

  • Explain, start and time the mission
  • Score on the same sheet everywhere
  • Check crew ID and status first
  • Record the 20% deduction if you played wanted
Control

Event control the people who can stop it

They own the format, the permissions, the target lists and the tie-breaks. They publish the top three at the end of every round, authorise any exception, and can pause or end the day.

  • Publish the target list each round
  • Authorise any police action off-list
  • Hold the scoring rubric and disputes
  • Confirm final scores and qualifiers
Story

Story & experience the atmosphere department

The opening briefing, the announcements, the characters at the stations and the reason a scoring board feels like a city instead of a quiz.

  • Opening story, rules and round calls
  • Character roles and voice
  • Keep the day moving
Logistics

Logistics & safety boring on purpose

Props, printouts, signage, water, crowd flow and the zone walk-through the night before. If a station is unsafe, it does not open — no matter how good the mission is.

  • Zones, signs and station set-up
  • Water and shade
  • Safety checks before every round

Three rules about scoring

  • The criteria are published before the event — no station invents a rubric at 13:00.

  • Timed missions record the clock. Ties are broken by time, then by missions banked.

Wanted? Your mission pays 20% less

The coordinator checks your status before the mission starts. If you are wanted you can still play — you simply keep 80% of what you earned. Or you walk to Dexter first.

File 05

THE TARGET LIST

Nobody gets targeted because a volunteer felt like it. There is one trigger, one verification step and one way out.

  1. 1

    A round ends

    Scores are locked for the round. The board updates and everybody can see it — including the crews about to have a hard next hour.

  2. 2

    The top three become targets

    The current top three crews on the leaderboard become the official police targets for the next round. Control shares their crew IDs and team-leader photos with the officer volunteers. The list can change after every single round.

  3. 3

    Police find the crew, verify the ID

    An officer approaches a listed crew inside an approved event zone, asks them to pause, and checks the crew ID. Off-list crews are left alone unless control authorises it for a specific rule.

  4. 4

    One photo of the team leader

    The officer takes or uploads a clear photo of the team leader through the event website. The site links the photo to that crew ID and shows the wanted poster on the team page and the operator view.

  5. 5

    Wanted — until you fix it

    Verified ID plus photo equals wanted. From that moment every mission you play pays 20% less, until you clear it.

  6. $

    Dexter clears it for $100

    Walk to Dexter's desk, pay the 100 in-game cash clearance fee, and the status is removed on the site — logged, timed and announced. From the next mission you score full points again.

Why this is fair

  • The trigger is performance, not preference — top three of the board, nothing else.
  • A crew is only wanted after its ID is verified. A photo alone proves nothing.
  • Officers cannot mark crews off the current list without control authorising it.
  • The fee is fixed and published, so clearing status is a plan, not a negotiation.
  • The target list changes every round. Nobody is hunted all day.
Privacy

About that photo

Leader photos exist for one thing: the internal wanted board. Access is restricted to event operators, participants are told this at registration, and every image is deleted after the event.

// do not want your photo taken at all? Say so at registration. Your crew is verified by crew ID instead, and nothing about your scoring changes.

What police never do

  • No chasing, no running at a crew
  • No touching, blocking or surrounding anybody
  • No patrolling outside the approved zones
  • No photos of crews that are not on the list

THE RULES

Nine of them. Rule 5 is the one that ends days, and it is not about winning.

VICE CITY · EVENT CONTROL · RULE SET VP-11 READ BEFORE YOU REGISTER
  1. Crews of 4–5, all with a valid Chitkara ID, using one crew ID for the whole event. A sixth member is not enthusiasm, it is a rules breach.
  2. Every crew gets the same missions and the same time limits. If a station is running long, control adjusts it for everybody at once, not for one crew.
  3. Station coordinators' instructions are the station's rules. Score entries can be reviewed by the control team if you dispute them — afterwards, at the desk.
  4. No sharing answers, sabotaging another crew, tampering with props or disturbing other teams. This is a competition, not a turf war.
  5. No running, pushing, grabbing, chasing or blocking pathways. Not at a station, not from the police, not towards a clue. A crew that does this is finished early, not penalised.
  6. Stay inside the approved event zones and respect regular campus activity. Classes, exams and staff corridors outrank the game.
  7. Late submissions are not accepted. When a mission's time window closes, it closes — for everyone.
  8. Control may deduct points or disqualify a crew for cheating, unsafe behaviour or repeated disruption. The decision is announced, not debated.
  9. Police photos and wanted status are part of the game only. Photos are used on the internal wanted board, restricted to operators and deleted after the event. Any participant may opt out of photo use — their crew is then verified by crew ID and scored exactly the same way.
// assistance

Any crew can stop playing and report a problem to a badge-holder at any moment. Injury, harassment, an unfair station, a phone that died — say so, immediately. The day exists for you, not the other way round.

Final scores are checked at 15:00 and announced by 15:30.

QUESTIONS
THE CITY ASKS

The questions the organisers have already answered three times in the group chat.

Do I need a gaming rig, a console or GTA VI?

No. Nobody plays the game on a screen. This is a real-world challenge across campus with a web platform keeping score. A charged phone and comfortable shoes are the equipment list.

Do I have to be a coder?

No. Tech & logic is one of five categories. Physical, creative, clue hunt and strategy reward communication, memory, nerve and judgement. Build a crew that covers all five.

Is the score cash or points?

Points. Completion up to 60, accuracy up to 25, speed up to 15, an optional bonus task for +10, and −5 for every hint. In-game cash is a separate resource you spend — mostly on Dexter.

Why would I want to be top of the board?

Because the top three crews become the police targets for the next round. Being first is worth points and costs you freedom, which is the entire tension of the day.

What exactly happens when I'm wanted?

An officer verifies your crew ID against the current target list, photographs your team leader, and the site marks you WANTED. Every mission you then play pays 80% of what you earned, until Dexter clears the status for $100 in-game.

Can I refuse to be photographed?

Yes — say so at registration. Your crew is verified by crew ID instead, your scoring is identical, and no photo is taken or stored. This is rule 9, not a favour.

Are the police allowed to touch or chase us?

Never. No chasing, grabbing, blocking or running at a crew, and officers stay inside the approved zones. If an officer breaks that, report it to control immediately; the crew is not at fault.

Can we team up with another crew?

Inside a mission that says so. Otherwise you are five people against a clock, and another crew's answers are their answers.

How many crews get into the Big Heist?

Five — the top five on the board after Round 3, announced at 14:15. Nobody is eliminated before that: every crew plays all three rounds and every point counts.

My phone died. Am I out?

No. Report to any station or checkpoint: your crew is verified against your ID and control's copy of your crew ID. Keep a screenshot of your crew ID for exactly this reason.

Is any of this money real?

None of it. Cash, fees and the opening wallet are fictional numbers inside the platform. Nothing can be exchanged for money, food or marks, and if somebody offers that, tell control.

What if it rains, or a zone gets unsafe?

Control can pause the day, move stations indoors or end gameplay. It goes out on the board and the speakers at the same moment, and it is not negotiable.

Still deciding?

One day. One campus. One story nobody retells the same way.
