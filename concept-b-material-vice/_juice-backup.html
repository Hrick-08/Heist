    01 · Briefing

    A campus, re-dressed

    No cosplay contest, no quiz night. A full-day, live, refereed game where the leaderboard is the story of the day and every rule is printed before the first mission starts.

    • Crews, not individuals

      Four or five students, one registration, one crew ID. That ID is how a station verifies you, how points land, and how control knows who to send the police to.

      4–5per crew

    • Three rounds of missions

      Starters, then the main set, then a final round. Short missions — three to eight minutes — so one crew can watch a whole board move in a day.

      3rounds

    • A police target list

      After every round the top three crews become official targets. Get photographed and verified, and every mission after that pays 20% less.

      −20%while wanted

    • Cash you can spend, points you win on

      Every crew starts with the same in-game wallet. Cash buys clearance from Dexter and wins the strategy missions. It does not buy the win — points do.

      110max per mission

    What we borrowed from GTA — and what we did not

    • Borrowed: the neon city, the wanted board, a crew, a broker, one big final job.
    • Not borrowed: the crime. Nothing here is illegal, hidden or dangerous — every mission is supervised, walked, and safe on a real campus.
    • Non-negotiable: published scoring, no contact between players, staff can stop the game at any moment, and every photo taken during the event is deleted after it.

    02 · Crew & crew ID

    Assemble a crew

    One registration per team. Tell us the crew name, the four or five names, and who the team leader is — that leader is the face on the wanted board if you top the chart.

    1. 1

      Pick four or five and a name

      A crew is 4–5 students with valid Chitkara IDs. Pick a name a speaker can read out loudly — it goes on the board, the target list and the trophy photo.

    2. 2

      Check in, collect the crew ID

      At 09:00 the crew gets one team ID, hears the story, the schedule and the scoring sheet, and gets the live board on a phone. Screenshot the ID: a dead phone should not end your day.

    3. 3

      Name a team leader for the poster

      If your crew lands on the target list, an officer photographs the team leader and uploads it to the site, which links the photo to your crew ID.

      Photos are used only for the internal wanted board, and every image is deleted after the event. Prefer no photo at all? Say so at registration — your crew is verified by ID instead and nothing about your scoring changes.

    4. 4

      Same missions, same clock, same wallet

      Every crew runs the same mission set with the same time limits and the same opening cash. Scoring criteria are published before the event; no station invents a rubric at 13:00.

    03 · Staffing

    The city has employees

    Every role you meet today is played by the organising team. Nobody improvises the rules, and nobody touches you.

    Police · the biggest role

    Vice City PD is run by volunteers

    Volunteer officers are game operators, not law enforcement. They patrol approved areas only, check a crew ID against the current target list, ask that crew to pause, photograph the team leader, and set the status in the site. That is the whole interaction — ninety seconds, then you play on.

    • Never chase, grab or block a crew
    • Verify the ID before marking anyone wanted
    • Approved zones only — no stairs, no parking decks
    • Dexter

      The clearance desk

      One desk, one broker, one fixed price. Pay $100 in-game and the wanted status is removed — logged, timed, announced — so the next mission pays full points.

    • Coordinators

      Station referees

      Two per station minimum. They check the crew ID and wanted status before the clock starts, judge against the printed checklist, and enter points while the memory is fresh.

    • Control room

      One channel, one clock

      Round calls, target list publication, corrections and the stop button. If control calls the game, the game stops — everywhere, immediately.

    06 · Target list

    Being famous is expensive

    Nobody gets targeted because a volunteer felt like it. One trigger, one verification step, one way out — and the list changes after every round.

    1. Round ends

      Scores lock, the board updates, and everybody can see who is about to have a hard hour.

    2. Top three are listed

      The current top three crews become the official police targets. Control shares crew IDs and leader photos with the officers.

    3. ID verified

      An officer approaches a listed crew in an approved zone, asks them to pause, and checks the crew ID.

    4. One leader photo

      The officer uploads a clear photo of the team leader. The site links it to that crew ID and publishes the poster.

    5. WANTED

      Verified ID plus photo equals wanted. Every mission after that pays 20% less — until you clear it.

    Pay Dexter, or eat the 20%?

    This is the real decision of the day. Put your numbers in.

    Why this is fair
    • The trigger is performance, not preference — top three of the board.
    • Wanted only after the ID is verified. A photo alone proves nothing.
    • Officers cannot mark crews off the current list without control authorising it.
    • The fee is fixed and published, so clearing status is a plan, not a negotiation.
    • The list changes every round. Nobody is hunted all day.
    Privacy

    About that photo

    Leader photos exist for one thing: the internal wanted board. Access is limited to event operators, participants are told this at registration, and every image is deleted after the event.

    Opting out changes nothing about your scoring — your crew is verified by crew ID instead.

      12 · Control room

      How the day is driven

      One operator channel, one spreadsheet that mirrors the board, and a stop button that beats every schedule on the wall.

      TimeWhat control does
      09:00Registration live. Crews created in the platform, wallet opened, photo consent recorded.
      09:30Rules briefing on the main screen. Scoring sheet read aloud, once, for everyone.
      10:00Round 1 opened. Stations start accepting attempts; points stream to the board.
      11:30Round 1 closed. Top three published as targets and pushed to officer phones.
      11:45Round 2 opened with the new target list in force.
      12:45Board frozen for lunch. Corrections window open — every edit needs control approval.
      13:15Round 3 opened. Dexter desk reminder on the channel: fee ends with the round.
      14:15Final mission points locked. Top five announced and moved to the heist staging area.
      14:30Big Heist live on the main screen with completion times.
      15:00Final score = mission points + heist bonus − penalties. Prizes, photos, dispersal by 15:30.

      Guardrails · printed on the staff sheet

      • Photo privacy

        Leader photos exist for the wanted board and nothing else. Operator access only, crews are told at registration, and every photo is deleted after the event. Opted out? The officer verifies the ID and logs a text description instead.

      • Safety

        Walked, never run. No chasing, no contact, no stunts, no restricted areas, water at every physical station. Control stops the game for weather, crowd density or any injury — nobody votes on it.

      • Money hygiene

        No real money changes hands anywhere in the event, Dexter included. The wallet log and the clearance ledger are reviewed after the awards, and any correction is posted publicly.

      Not decided yet

      The brief leaves these open. They freeze at the last core meeting and this page is updated the same hour — anything published before then is marked as a proposal.

      • Final mission list and the category mix per round
      • Total points budget per round and per category
      • Opening wallet size (1 000 is the working number)
      • How many police volunteers we can actually field
      • Sponsor prizes, and whether any of them are real

      14 · Rules & FAQ

      Read this once, thank us later

      The same nine rules are on the briefing screen, the station cards and the control-room wall.

      Rule sheet
      1. Respect the event zone. Normal campus rules keep applying.
      2. No running, no rough play, no stunts. Missions are designed to be walked.
      3. No touching other crews, staff, or officers. Ever.
      4. One crew ID per team. Points land against that ID only.
      5. Coordinators' scoring is final. Corrections go through control, in writing.
      6. Wanted crews keep 80% of a mission's points until Dexter clears them for $100.
      7. Hints cost 5 points each. Take one; that is what they are for.
      8. In-game cash is fictional. No real money is ever exchanged.
      9. Leader photos are for the internal wanted board only, and are deleted after the event. Opt out at registration if you prefer.
      Do I need a team to register?

      Yes — one registration per crew of 4–5. Individual entries get pooled into a crew at check-in if there is space, but you will not have picked your teammates.

      How much running is involved?

      None. Physical missions are walked and supervised. If a mission cannot be done without running, it does not make the shortlist.

      What happens if I get caught by the police?

      An officer checks your crew ID against the current target list. If it matches and your team leader photo is uploaded, you are wanted and every mission pays 20% less until you pay Dexter $100 or the list changes next round.

      Do I have to be photographed?

      No. Say so at registration and your crew is verified by crew ID only. Nothing about your scoring, missions or heist eligibility changes.

      Is the money real?

      No. Every crew starts with the same in-game wallet (proposed $1,000). It can buy a clearance or win a strategy mission. It cannot buy anything real.

      What do I win with all this cash?

      Cash is a tool; points win. Completion 60, quality 25, speed 15, bonus task +10, hint −5, wanted −20%, heist up to +150.

      How do we get into the Big Heist?

      Be in the top five after the final mission round at 14:15. Then finish the heist: +150 for first across the line, +120, +100, +75 for finishing.

      Phone dies — do we lose the day?

      No. Station coordinators can look your crew up by ID, and control keeps a printed mirror of the board all day. Screenshot your ID anyway.

      Where do I submit a mission idea?

      The Mission lab above. It builds a review card you paste into the organising channel — every team member is expected to bring one or two.
